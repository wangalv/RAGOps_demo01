One of the three kinds of [[public-health-organisation]] (see also [[local-health-district]],
[[statutory-health-corporation]]). Non-government (often religious/charitable) organisations
recognised under s.62, listed in Schedule 3 with their specific recognised establishments/
services — recognition is partial (only those listed establishments/services), not organisation-
wide.

## What makes AHOs different

Unlike LHDs and statutory health corporations, almost every action the Crown can take regarding
an AHO requires **the AHO's own consent**:

- Recognition/amendment of Schedule 3 entries (s.62(4)).
- Declaring it a "declared affiliated health organisation" (s.62A(5)).
- Recognising it as part of a network (s.62B(5)).
- Transferring its establishments/services to a LHD/statutory health corporation (s.64(2)).
- Ministerial direction to acquire services under s.126G (s.126H).

## Declared vs. non-declared

- **Declared** AHOs cannot employ staff directly (s.62A(3)) — staff come via the
  [[nsw-health-service]], same as LHDs/statutory health corporations.
- **Non-declared** AHOs may employ their own staff; a parallel version of the Chapter 9 Part 2
  misconduct-reporting regime is applied to them by modification (s.63A).

## Key powers

- May make by-laws with Minister approval (management, patient services, staff discipline,
  records, meetings) — visiting-practitioner by-laws need Medical Services Committee advice
  (s.63).
- Good-faith liability protection for the org, governing body, members, and agents (s.67).
