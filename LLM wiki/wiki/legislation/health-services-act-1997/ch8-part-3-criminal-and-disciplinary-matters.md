Mandatory disclosure and termination regime for [[visiting-practitioner]] serious sex/violence
offences and professional misconduct findings — mirrors [[ch9-part-2-criminal-and-disciplinary-matters]]
for NSW Health Service staff.

## Sections

| s. | Heading | Summary |
|---|---|---|
| 99 | Duty to report criminal/disciplinary matters | Practitioner must report, within 7 days, a charge/conviction for a [[serious-sex-or-violence-offence]], or a misconduct finding, to the chief executive. |
| 99A | Duty of chief executive to report conduct | Chief executive must report suspected misconduct to the relevant professional council (Medical Council or Dental Council of NSW); dual-reporting satisfied by compliance with either regime. |
| 100 | Termination in serious sex/violence offence cases | Chief executive must notify [[health-secretary]] within 30 days of a conviction, with reasons for proposed termination/retention and any practitioner submissions; Health Secretary confirms or directs otherwise; org may terminate on its own initiative only after Health Secretary confirmation; **must** terminate if directed. |
| 101 | Protection of patients/clients/children paramount | Paramount consideration in termination decisions under this Part. |
| 102 | No compensation for termination | No compensation payable for termination under this Part. |
| 103 | Appeal rights | Right to appeal under Pt 4 generally, **except** where termination is based on a serious sex/violence offence against a minor or a patient/client during practice. |
| 104 | Effect of Part on other rights | Doesn't affect other rights re non-disclosure or the underlying conduct; doesn't affect Criminal Records Act 1991 spent-convictions protection. |

## Key obligations and powers

- **Obligation** (s.99) — practitioner must report a charge/conviction/finding within 7 days.
- **Obligation** (s.99A(1)) — chief executive must report suspected misconduct to the relevant
  professional council.
- **Obligation** (s.100(1)–(2)) — chief executive must notify Health Secretary within 30 days of
  becoming aware of a conviction.
- **Obligation** (s.100(6)) — org **must** terminate if Health Secretary directs.
- **Restriction** (s.100(5)) — org may terminate on its own initiative only after Health Secretary
  confirms the proposal.
- **Prohibition-adjacent** (s.103(2)) — no appeal right where termination follows conviction for
  an offence against a minor or a patient/client during practice.
