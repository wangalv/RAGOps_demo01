Source: [[health-services-act-1997]]. Regulates the NSW public health system: local health
districts, statutory health corporations, affiliated health organisations, ambulance services,
visiting practitioners, and the NSW Health Service (the centralised employer for public health
staff).

## Key parties

- [[minister]] — the Minister for Health (and related health portfolios); holds directive,
  approval, and appointment powers throughout.
- [[health-secretary]] — Secretary of the Ministry of Health; the day-to-day administrative head
  of the public health system, with the largest single concentration of functions in the Act.
- [[local-health-district]], [[statutory-health-corporation]], [[affiliated-health-organisation]]
  — the three kinds of [[public-health-organisation]].
- [[nsw-health-service]] — the centralised employer of public health system staff (Chapter 9).
- [[ambulance-service-of-nsw]] — staff of the NSW Health Service employed in ambulance services
  (Chapter 5A).
- [[health-administration-corporation]] — Crown body that can itself control public hospitals.
- [[australian-medical-association-nsw]] — statutory counterparty for visiting medical officer
  contract disputes (Chapter 8 Pt 2).

## Penalties overview

Most substantive provisions are civil/administrative (directions, approvals, terminations) rather
than criminal. Criminal offences with penalties are concentrated in:

- s.67E Unauthorised provision of ambulance transport — 50 penalty units
- s.67F Unauthorised collections (for ambulance services) — 50 penalty units
- s.67G False calls for ambulance services — 50 penalty units
- s.134 Obstructing/hindering Minister, Health Secretary, authorised officer, or other public
  official — 100 penalty units
- s.140(5) Regulations may create offences punishable up to 5 penalty units
- Proceedings for offences under the Act are dealt with summarily before the Local Court (s.137)
